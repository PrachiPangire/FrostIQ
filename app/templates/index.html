{# ------------------------------------------------------------- 2. PROBLEM #}
01 · The problem

A journey can change everything

A temperature-sensitive medicine begins its journey in a controlled environment. Over hours of transport, ordinary events can push it warmer than intended. Scroll through them and watch the cooler.

  1. Packed in a cooled container, the medicine starts within its permitted range.

  2. Warm air rushes in during loading or a stop. A short opening causes a quick spike.

  3. If the cooling unit stops, nothing pulls the temperature back down.

  4. A failing compressor doesn't stop at once. The temperature creeps up over hours.

  5. Hot weather or a sunny loading bay means more heat leaks through the walls.

So is the medicine ruined?

Not necessarily. The real risk depends on the specific medicine, its permitted storage conditions and how long it was exposed. A single reading above a line doesn't automatically make every product unusable. That judgement belongs to documented requirements and qualified people.

02 · The journey

From cold store to clinic

A shipment passes through several hands. A small sensor in the container logs the temperature every 5 minutes along the whole route. Scroll to move the cooler along it.

  1. Cold store The shipment is packed and cooled.
  2. Truck Doors open at stops; the cooling runs on vehicle power.
  3. Transfer hub Waiting on a loading bay, sometimes in the sun.
  4. Clinic The container arrives. Was it kept cold enough all the way?

{# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 4. RISK #}
03 · Temperature risk

When does warm become a risk?

FrostIQ compares each recorded reading with a threshold: {{ '%g' % threshold_c }} °C by default, an experimental choice for this simulation. A crossing means a reading strictly above it.

  • Normal Below the threshold with more than 1 °C to spare.
  • Warning Still below, but within 1 °C, or the classifier flags a likely crossing.
  • Above threshold A recorded reading went over the line. That is observed, not predicted.

Illustration: Normal: well below the threshold

observed A temperature that was actually recorded.
predicted A model's guess about the future, which can be wrong.

{# ----------------------------------------------------------------- 5. HOW #}
04 · How the ML models work

From readings to two predictions

  1. 1
    Observe Recorded readings so far: never future ones.
  2. 2
    Describe the trend 15 features: distance to the threshold, warming rate over 15–60 minutes, recent highs and lows, past excursions.
  3. 3a
    Classification A RandomForestClassifier answers yes or no: will it cross within {{ '%g' % horizon_min }} minutes?
  4. 3b
    Regression Experimental A HistGradientBoostingRegressor estimates how many minutes until a crossing.
  5. 4
    Understand The website shows both, labels them as predictions, and explains when there's no valid answer.

Both models were trained on {% if card %}{{ card.train_journeys }}{% else %}the training{% endif %} journeys only. Whole journeys are held out for testing, so no journey is in both.

Readings flow into the model core and out as two outputs: classification and regression

{# -------------------------------------------------------------- 8. LIMITS #}
07 · Limitations

An honest look at what this project is

Simulated, not validated

All journeys and readings are simulated. FrostIQ has not been validated for real medicine or vaccine shipments and must not be used for safety decisions.

Predictions can be wrong

Some crossings are missed, some warnings are false alarms, and the minutes estimate is experimental. A prediction is not a guarantee.

Built with

Python, pandas, NumPy and scikit-learn for the ML; Flask and Jinja for the website; three.js, GSAP, Chart.js and vanilla JavaScript for the interface; pytest for testing.

Before real-world use

Real sensor data, product-specific limits from experts, evaluation per excursion on fresh data, calibrated scores, and independent safety and regulatory review.
